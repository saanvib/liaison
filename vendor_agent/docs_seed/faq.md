# AuroraPrep RUO Kit — Frequently Asked Questions

**For Research Use Only (RUO). Not for use in diagnostic procedures.**

## General

**Q: What is the AuroraPrep RUO Kit used for?**
A: It purifies total nucleic acid (DNA and RNA, or DNA-only with the optional RNase step) from research samples such as cultured cells, fresh/frozen tissue, and research-grade plasma or serum. See the datasheet for full performance characteristics.

**Q: What sizes does the kit come in?**
A: A 50-prep size (AP-2200-50), a 200-prep size (AP-2200-200), and a 10-prep starter size (AP-2200-10).

**Q: Can I use this kit with whole blood or saliva?**
A: The kit has not been validated for whole blood or saliva in our internal studies; validated sample types are tissue, cultured cells, and research-grade plasma/serum. If you need to work with other sample types, start with a small-scale test before committing a full experiment to the kit.

**Q: How long does the protocol take?**
A: Approximately 35 minutes for 50 samples using the standard lysis protocol (excluding the optional overnight lysis variant for difficult tissue).

**Q: What's the shelf life of an unopened kit?**
A: 18 months from the date of manufacture; check the lot-specific expiration date printed on the box.

**Q: Is reconstituted Proteinase K stable?**
A: Yes, for up to 6 months at -20°C, avoiding repeated freeze-thaw cycles.

## Downstream use

**Q: Is the purified DNA/RNA compatible with qPCR and NGS library prep?**
A: Yes, it's compatible with standard qPCR and most commercial NGS library prep kits. We haven't internally validated every third-party downstream kit, so confirm compatibility with your specific workflow.

**Q: What A260/A280 purity should I expect?**
A: Typically 1.8-2.0 for DNA-enriched preps; see the validation report for full data by sample type.

**Q: Why is my plasma yield lower and more variable than tissue yield?**
A: Plasma cell-free nucleic acid content naturally varies more by donor and freeze-thaw history; our validation data shows a higher coefficient of variation for plasma (18%) than for tissue (11%) or cultured cells (9%).

## Troubleshooting

**Q: My yield is lower than expected. What should I check first?**
A: Confirm your input amount is within the stated range (10-200 mg tissue, up to 5x10^6 cells, 200 µL plasma), confirm ethanol was added at the binding and both wash steps, and consider extending lysis time for tough tissue.

**Q: My spin column keeps clogging. What should I do?**
A: Reduce the input amount and make sure tissue is fully homogenized before adding Lysis Buffer AL.
